What we build with generative models

Most projects start with one valuable feature. We agree the quality bar and how we will test it before the first prompt.

  • 01

    Drafting and summaries

    First drafts and short summaries built from records already in your system, editable before anything is sent.

  • 02

    Document generation

    Reports and proposals assembled from your templates. The model fills the sections that need judgement and fixed clauses stay fixed.

  • 03

    Image generation and editing

    Visuals within style references and content filters, from resizing campaign assets to text-to-image drafts.

  • 04

    Reading images and audio

    Features that read photos and scanned forms or transcribe calls, feeding the same structured data.

  • 05

    Review queues

    Bulk generation that a person signs off, with approval screens and a history of what changed.

  • 06

    Brand voice and structured output

    Tone guides, banned phrases and examples steer the style, and schemas make every response fit your data.

How we keep generated content safe

A fluent draft can still be wrong. The checks sit between the model and your customer.

  • Grounded

    Facts come from your records

    Names, figures and dates are inserted from your data, not recalled by the model.

  • Checked

    Banned claims and drift checks

    Each draft is checked against your rules and the source facts before an editor sees it.

  • Person

    Editor approval where it counts

    Anything that costs money or trust if wrong is approved by a person before it goes out.

  • Logged

    Provenance on published content

    Each item keeps its sources, checks and approver, and is labelled as AI-assisted where needed.

Common questions about this work

How do you stop the model making things up?

We give the model the facts instead of asking it to recall them, check outputs against a schema and test cases, and keep a person approving high-stakes content. The risk can be reduced a lot but not removed.

Can the output match our brand voice?

Within limits, yes. A tone guide, approved examples and a banned-phrase list steer the style, and we test outputs against them. If prompting cannot get close, a fine-tuned smaller model is an option.

Who owns the prompts and the generated content?

Terms are agreed in the contract. Rights in generated content also depend on each model provider's terms, which we review with you before choosing a provider.

Is our data used to train the model?

We use business API tiers where providers state they do not train on your inputs, and we minimise personal data sent to a model. Your own data protection team signs off the processing.

How is this different from an agent or a chatbot?

A generative feature produces content when asked, such as a draft or a report. An agent chooses steps and calls tools. A chatbot holds a conversation. Many products use a mix, and we scope which one a problem needs.
