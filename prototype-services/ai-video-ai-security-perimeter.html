What the cameras watch for on site

Security cameras produce far more footage than anyone can watch. We agree which events matter before building.

  • 01

    Perimeter and fence lines

    People or vehicles crossing a line or entering a restricted area, with a still frame sent to your control room or on-call contact.

  • 02

    After-hours entry

    Movement at doors, gates and yards outside agreed hours, so the right person looks at it while it is happening.

  • 03

    Loitering and tailgating

    People remaining in an agreed area for longer than expected, or following someone through a controlled door.

  • 04

    Doors and gates left open

    Doors held open or gates left unlocked longer than the agreed time, flagged to the site contact.

  • 05

    Vehicles in the wrong place

    Vehicles stopped in fire lanes, loading zones or restricted areas for longer than agreed.

  • 06

    Searchable incident history

    Ask for everything at the rear gate between 22:00 and 05:00 last week and get the clips with a short summary.

Rules your team sets and changes

You decide what the agent looks for, who is told and what response follows. Rules are written down before launch.

  • Person

    Areas and hours that count

    Your security lead marks the restricted areas and the allowed hours, with exceptions for planned work.

  • Person

    Who is told, and when

    Routing by area and time of day, with an escalation route if nobody responds.

  • Person

    Response stays with people

    The agent never calls the police, sounds an alarm or confronts anyone. A person decides each response.

  • Person

    No identification by default

    Rules detect presence and movement, not identity. Anything more needs a lawful basis agreed first.

  • Person

    What is kept as evidence

    Your team decides which clips are retained after an incident, and for how long.

Privacy and people for security cameras

Security cameras record staff, visitors and sometimes the public. These points are agreed before a camera goes live.

  • 01

    Signage that faces the public

    Clear signs at entrances and along the perimeter say that cameras are in use, why, and who to contact.

  • 02

    No facial recognition by default

    We do not add facial recognition. Live facial recognition is high risk under UK GDPR and would need a lawful basis and assessment first.

  • 03

    Public space and neighbours

    Camera views are masked so they do not cover neighbouring property or public footpaths more than necessary.

  • 04

    Blurring, retention and assessment

    Blurring and retention limits are set per camera, and we help with a data protection impact assessment.

Common questions about this work

Can it work with our existing CCTV and recorder?

Usually, yes. If your cameras or recorder can stream over RTSP or ONVIF, we can read them. We check coverage, lighting and night-time image quality on your own footage first, because they decide how reliable alerts will be. We also note any blind spots up front.

How many false alarms should we expect?

It depends on the scene, the weather and the rule. Foxes, shadows and headlights can all trigger alerts. We test on your own footage, show you what it flags wrongly and tune thresholds before launch. A person reviews every alert.

Does it use facial recognition?

Not by default, and we do not recommend it for most sites. The rules look for people, vehicles and movement. Facial recognition is high risk under UK GDPR and would need a lawful basis, an assessment and your adviser's view first.

Will it call the police or sound an alarm?

No. It alerts your control room or on-call contact with a still frame or clip. A person decides the response. This keeps judgement with people who can check the evidence and avoids automatic escalation from a mistaken alert. Your own procedures decide who is called and when.

Is this allowed under UK GDPR?

It can be, with a lawful basis, a data protection impact assessment, clear signage and, for staff, consultation. We help with the assessment and the rollout plan. Legal advice on your own case should come from your adviser. Your data protection officer, if you have one, stays involved.
