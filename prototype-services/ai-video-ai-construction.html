What the cameras watch for on site

Sites change week to week, so we start with the areas that matter most and agree the rules before building.

  • 01

    Hard hats and hi-vis

    PPE checked in the areas where it is required, with an alert and a still frame for the supervisor on that part of the site.

  • 02

    Exclusion zones

    People entering marked areas such as open excavations, lifting zones or edges, flagged while someone can still intervene.

  • 03

    Plant and people

    Excavators, dumpers and delivery vehicles moving near workers, logged as near-misses with a short clip.

  • 04

    Out-of-hours entry

    People or vehicles on site outside agreed hours, with a still frame for whoever is on call.

  • 05

    Gate and delivery logs

    Vehicle arrivals at the site entrance logged with time and a still frame, to compare against booked deliveries.

  • 06

    Progress snapshots

    Regular time-stamped views of agreed areas, so managers can look back at what was done and when.

Rules your team sets and changes

You decide what the agent looks for, who is told and how quickly. Rules are written down before launch and updated as the site changes.

  • Person

    Where each zone is

    Your H&S lead marks exclusion and PPE zones on the camera view and moves them as work progresses.

  • Person

    Working and non-working hours

    The times that count as out of hours are yours, with exceptions for planned late work.

  • Person

    Who is told, and when

    Routing by area and shift, with an on-call route for out-of-hours alerts.

  • Person

    Distance for plant near workers

    Set per machine type, so the limit for a dumper can differ from the limit for a crane.

  • Person

    What a person must review

    Anything affecting a worker is reviewed by a named person before any follow-up.

Privacy and people on a building site

Sites include employees, subcontractors, visitors and sometimes passers-by. These points are agreed before a camera goes live.

  • 01

    Cameras aimed at the site

    We recommend views that cover the work area and entrance, masking neighbouring property and public footpaths where possible.

  • 02

    Subcontractors and visitors

    Signage at the entrance and induction briefings tell everyone cameras are in use and why.

  • 03

    Video stays with you

    Processing runs on site equipment or in your private cloud, so raw video is not sent to applyd.ai.

  • 04

    Blurring, retention and assessment

    Blurring and retention limits are set per camera, and we help with a data protection impact assessment and consultation.

Common questions about this work

Can it run on temporary site cameras?

Yes, if the cameras can stream over RTSP or ONVIF, or a small edge box can be placed on site. Connectivity on site varies, so we plan where processing runs and what happens if the connection drops. We agree the fallback with your site team.

Does weather or light affect it?

It can. Rain, glare, dust and low light all change what a camera sees. We test on your own footage at different times of day, show you where it misses and tune the rules. Some views may suit only certain checks.

What if the site layout keeps changing?

Zones are drawn on the camera view and your team can move them as the work moves. We agree who is allowed to change them, so an exclusion zone is never removed by mistake. Changes are logged, so you can see who moved what and when.

Will it be used to monitor individual workers?

Not by default. Most rules need to know that a person is in a zone or without a hard hat, not who. A person reviews alerts, and we recommend agreeing the approach with workers and subcontractors before launch. Workers can raise concerns through the same route.

Is this allowed under UK GDPR?

It can be, with a lawful basis, a data protection impact assessment, clear signage and consultation with workers. We help with the assessment and the rollout plan. Legal advice on your own case should come from your adviser. Your data protection officer, if you have one, stays involved.
