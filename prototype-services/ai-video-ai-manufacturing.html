What the cameras watch for on site

Each project starts with a few cameras and a few rules. We agree what counts as an event before anything is built.

  • 01

    PPE near machines

    Hi-vis, safety glasses, hearing protection and gloves checked in the zones where they are required, with a still frame sent to the person who can act.

  • 02

    Guards and access doors

    Machine guards left open or access doors propped while a cell is running, flagged while someone can still walk over and close them.

  • 03

    Line stoppages

    A line that has stopped longer than an agreed time, with the operator asked for the reason so it is recorded instead of guessed.

  • 04

    People near moving plant

    Pedestrians inside the marked distance of forklifts, robots or conveyors, logged as near-misses with a short clip.

  • 05

    Task and step checks

    Whether a lock-out was applied or a pack-out step was done in the right order, with the clip kept as evidence for the audit.

  • 06

    Visible defects

    Surface marks, misaligned labels or missing parts that a camera can see, passed to quality staff for a decision.

Rules your team sets and changes

You decide what the agent looks for, who is told and how quickly. Rules are written down before launch.

  • Person

    Which zones need which PPE

    Your safety lead lists the zones and the equipment required in each, and changes the list when the site changes.

  • Person

    How long a stop is too long

    Set per line, so a planned changeover is not treated like a breakdown.

  • Person

    Who is told, and when

    Routing by line, zone and shift, with a quieter route for out-of-hours alerts.

  • Person

    What counts as a near-miss

    The distance and the vehicle types are yours to set. The agent logs events that meet them.

  • Person

    What a person must review

    Anything that could affect a worker is reviewed by a named person before any follow-up.

Privacy and people on the shop floor

Watching people at work needs care. These points are agreed with you and your workers before a camera goes live.

  • 01

    Rules about PPE, not about people

    Most rules only need to know that someone is present and what they are wearing, not who they are.

  • 02

    Video stays on your site

    Processing runs on your own servers or private cloud, so raw video is not sent to applyd.ai.

  • 03

    Blurring and retention

    Faces can be blurred in stored clips and dashboards, and clips are deleted after the number of days you set.

  • 04

    Consultation and assessment

    We help with a data protection impact assessment and recommend signage and talking to workers or their representatives first.

Common questions about this work

Can it use the cameras we already have on the line?

Usually yes. If your cameras or recorder can stream over RTSP or ONVIF, we can read them. We check angle, lighting and resolution on your own footage first, because they decide which rules a camera can support reliably. We also note any blind spots up front.

How does it know why a line stopped?

It does not guess. The camera shows that the line has stopped, then the agent asks the operator for the reason and records the reply. Where you can share machine signals, those can confirm the stop and feed your MES or maintenance tool.

Will it be used to discipline workers?

It is not built for that. The agent raises events and a person reviews them before anything happens. We recommend using alerts for safety and process fixes, and agreeing that approach with your workers and their representatives before launch. Workers can raise concerns through the same route.

How accurate will it be on our site?

That depends on the cameras, the lighting and the rule. We test on your own footage before launch, show you where it misses and tune thresholds with you. No system is perfect, which is why a person reviews alerts. You see the results before anyone relies on them.

Is this allowed under UK GDPR?

It can be, with a lawful basis, a data protection impact assessment, clear signage and consultation with workers. We help you complete the assessment and plan the rollout. Legal advice on your own case should come from your adviser. Your data protection officer, if you have one, stays involved.
