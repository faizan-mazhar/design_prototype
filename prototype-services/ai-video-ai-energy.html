Vision agents for energy sites, with operators in charge

We build agents that watch the cameras already on your plant and remote sites, explain what is happening and alert the operator or control room that decides what to do.

What the cameras watch for on site

Hazardous sites need rules agreed with the people who run them. We settle what counts as an event before building.

  • 01

    PPE in hazardous areas

    Hard hats, hi-vis, gloves and other equipment checked where required, with a still frame sent to the person who can act.

  • 02

    Exclusion and permit zones

    People entering marked areas, or working in a zone without a permit on record, flagged to the control room.

  • 03

    Gauge and status reading

    Dials, indicator lights and equipment status read between rounds and logged, with unusual readings passed to an operator.

  • 04

    Visible leaks and spills

    Staining, pooling or visible vapour that a camera can see, raised for an operator to check in person.

  • 05

    Unmanned and remote sites

    Gate, fence and compound views checked for people or vehicles outside planned visits, with a still frame for the on-call contact.

  • 06

    Vehicle and lifting movements

    Vehicles in restricted routes or loads moving near people, logged with a short clip for review.

Rules your team sets and changes

You decide what the agent looks for, who is told and how quickly. Rules are written down before launch and reviewed with your safety team.

  • Person

    Zones and the permits they need

    Your site team defines the zones and what counts as a valid permit. The agent only compares what you give it.

  • Person

    Normal ranges for readings

    Engineers set the range for each gauge. Readings outside it go to an operator, not to an automatic response.

  • Person

    Who is told, and when

    Routing by area, shift and severity, with a separate on-call route for unmanned sites.

  • Person

    No control of plant

    The agent raises alerts only. It does not operate valves, switches or safety systems.

  • Person

    What a person must review

    Every alert about people or equipment is reviewed by a named person before any follow-up.

Privacy and people on plant and remote sites

Operators, contractors and visitors are all on camera. These points are agreed with the site before a camera goes live.

  • 01

    On your estate

    Processing runs on site servers or in your private cloud, so raw video is not sent to applyd.ai.

  • 02

    Limited connectivity

    Remote sites can process on the spot and send only short alerts and stills, rather than streaming everything.

  • 03

    Contractors and visitors

    Signage and induction briefings tell contractors and visitors that cameras are in use and why.

  • 04

    Blurring, retention and assessment

    Blurring and retention limits are set per camera, and we help with a data protection impact assessment and consultation.

Common questions about this work

Can it work on remote sites with poor connectivity?

Yes, in many cases. Processing can run on a small edge device at the site, sending only short alerts and still frames over whatever link exists. We plan what happens if the connection drops, and what is stored locally until it returns.

Does it replace our safety systems?

No. It is an extra pair of eyes that raises alerts for people to review. It does not control plant, and it is not a safety-rated system. Your existing safety instrumented systems and procedures stay as they are. Your engineers decide how any alert fits your procedures.

How well can it read gauges and status lights?

It depends on the camera angle, the lighting and the gauge type. We test on your own footage, show you what it reads reliably and what it misses, and keep a person in the loop for anything unusual. Where a reading cannot be trusted, we say so and leave it out.

Can it work in hazardous or explosive areas?

Cameras in classified areas must be suitably certified, and that is a matter for your engineers. We work with the cameras you already have installed and do not supply or certify hardware. Processing can sit outside the hazardous area. Your engineers remain responsible for equipment ratings.

Is this allowed under UK GDPR?

It can be, with a lawful basis, a data protection impact assessment, clear signage and consultation with workers. We help with the assessment and the rollout plan. Legal advice on your own case should come from your adviser. Your data protection officer, if you have one, stays involved.
