Cyber risk becomes operational risk

Industrial systems connect people, equipment and processes. Those connections also create pathways that need to be understood and protected. We help your team examine how systems communicate, who can access them and where security weaknesses could affect operations.

Start with a clearer picture. Build a practical path toward stronger protection.

First, understand what is connected

Effective protection starts with visibility into your assets and their role in the operation.

  • 01

    OT asset visibility

    Build an inventory of industrial devices, systems and relevant connections.

  • 02

    Network and communication mapping

    Understand how information moves between operational systems, enterprise networks and external services.

  • 03

    Operational criticality

    Identify which assets support essential processes and where disruption would have the greatest impact.

The outcome A clearer view of what needs protection and where to focus first.

Then, examine the paths into your operation

Access should reflect the work someone needs to perform, and the systems they are authorised to reach.

  • 01

    Remote and vendor access

    Review how employees, suppliers and maintenance providers connect to operational systems.

  • 02

    Network segmentation

    Assess boundaries between enterprise IT, operational networks and critical equipment environments.

  • 03

    Identity and permissions

    Review accounts, privileges, authentication and access responsibilities.

  • 04

    Configuration and exposure

    Identify unnecessary connections, insecure settings and potential exposure points.

The outcome Prioritised recommendations for strengthening access and reducing avoidable exposure.

Recognise changes that deserve investigation

A new device, an unexpected connection or unusual access activity can create questions your team needs to answer. OT security monitoring helps bring relevant observations into view, alongside the context needed for investigation.

  • 01

    Unexpected connections

    Identify communication that differs from the approved or established network pattern.

  • 02

    Unusual access activity

    Review unexpected login behaviour, remote sessions and access to sensitive systems.

  • 03

    Asset and configuration changes

    Surface relevant changes to monitored devices and configurations where suitable data is available.

  • 04

    Security event notifications

    Route observations to designated contacts through agreed dashboards and communication channels.

Monitoring coverage is defined around your systems, available data and operational requirements.

Turn findings into a practical security plan

A list of weaknesses is useful only when your team knows what to do next. We prioritise findings by their operational significance, exposure and remediation requirements.

  • 01

    Address the highest-priority risks

    Focus on issues that could have the greatest effect on critical systems and processes.

  • 02

    Plan changes around operations

    Consider equipment constraints, vendor requirements, maintenance windows and change-control procedures.

  • 03

    Prepare for an incident

    Define responsibilities, escalation paths and response procedures before an event demands them.

  • 04

    Review recovery readiness

    Assess backup arrangements, restoration procedures and the information needed to support recovery.

The outcome An actionable improvement plan with clear priorities and ownership.

Frameworks we work to

Assessments and improvement plans are structured around recognised industrial and national guidance, so your findings map onto the language your auditors, regulators and engineers already use.

  • 01

    IEC 62443

    Security for industrial automation and control systems. We use its ideas of zones and conduits and security levels to structure the review of network boundaries and access.

  • 02

    NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3

    The NIST Guide to OT Security, used as a reference for the risks and safeguards that suit operational technology.

  • 03

    NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0

    Our four steps line up with its functions: understand and assess map to Identify, strengthen to Protect, recognise to Detect, and prepare to Respond and Recover.

  • 04

    UK NCSC Cyber Assessment Framework

    Assessments can be aligned with the CAF, with the NIS Regulations 2018 and the EU NIS2 Directive in mind for operators of essential services. We help you prepare evidence for that work.

  • 05

    ISO/IEC 27001

    Where OT connects to your enterprise information security management system, we link findings to it.

  • 06

    MITRE ATT&CK for ICS

    Used to make monitoring threat-informed, by describing how attackers are known to act against industrial control systems.

Passive first, no disruption to running processes

Industrial systems are already in service, so we start by looking, not touching.

  • 01

    Non-intrusive discovery

    We begin with passive observation and the information you already hold, so running processes are not disturbed.

  • 02

    Change windows

    Anything that could affect operations is planned for agreed maintenance windows and your change-control procedures.

  • 03

    Vendor coordination

    Equipment vendors are involved where their support terms or system constraints apply.

Naming a framework here is not a claim of certification against it, and working to one does not make your organisation compliant. Compliance decisions rest with your auditor or regulator.

Common questions about this work

What does OT cybersecurity cover?

The networks, devices and control systems behind your industrial operation. We help you see what is connected, review who and what can reach it, bring relevant changes into view and plan how your team would respond.

How is this different from office IT security?

Industrial systems are already in service and support live work, so they come with equipment constraints, vendor requirements and maintenance windows. We plan around those instead of treating the plant like an office network.

Will you change our control systems?

We start by understanding the environment, not by changing it. Any change is planned around operations with your team, including your change-control procedures and vendor requirements.

Which standards do you work to?

Assessments and improvement plans are structured around IEC 62443, NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0, and can be aligned with the UK NCSC Cyber Assessment Framework. ISO/IEC 27001 is used where OT links to your enterprise security management, and MITRE ATT&CK for ICS informs monitoring.

Will this make us compliant?

Not by itself. We help you assess your position and prepare evidence against frameworks such as the NIS Regulations 2018 or NIS2, but compliance decisions rest with your auditor or regulator.

What do we get?

Depending on the stage: a clearer view of what needs protection, prioritised recommendations for access and exposure, monitoring arrangements and an improvement plan with clear priorities and ownership.

Can monitoring cover every system?

Coverage is defined around your systems, the data available and your operational requirements. Some systems may offer little to observe, and we say so rather than assume.

Is this a product or a service?

It is a service. We work with your team on assessment, recommendations and support for agreed security measures.

How do we start?

Start with an assessment, a remote-access concern or a critical system you want to protect. Book a demo and tell us about your OT environment and the security question your team needs to answer.

What does OT cybersecurity cover for an industrial site?

It protects the networks, devices and control systems that run the process. Work starts with passive discovery of what is connected, then reviews remote and vendor access, segmentation and permissions, monitors for unexpected changes and turns findings into a prioritised plan structured around IEC 62443, NIST SP 800-82 and the NCSC CAF.
